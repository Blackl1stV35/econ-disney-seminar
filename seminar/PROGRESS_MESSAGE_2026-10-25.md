## English

Dear Professor, our group would like to share our progress. The topic is now narrowed to financial appraisal only: "Scenario-Based Financial Feasibility and Break-Even Analysis of a Proposed Disney-Type Theme Park in Thailand" (earlier "Financial and Economic Appraisal of a Proposed Disney-Type Theme-Park Complex in Thailand: A Scenario Analysis"). We dropped the economic, fiscal and causal-effect questions. Our objective is to test whether a park-only operator can earn a return at an 8 percent cost of capital, and what capital cost and attendance it needs to break even. The hypothesis (H1) is that the park has a positive after-tax net present value. The research question is whether the proposed park is financially viable for an operator, and how sensitive the result is to capital cost, revenue, operating cost, project life and discount rate.

Our method is a preliminary financial cash flow model in the attached Excel file, where every input has a reference to its source on the Inputs sheet. The model covers a park-only capital cost of about THB 200 billion (a news figure, not verified), two construction years and a 20-year operating life. Revenue is ticket income plus on-site income per visit, using Hong Kong Disneyland prices and an Oriental Land on-site benchmark converted at the Bank of Thailand rate. Operating cost is 77 percent of revenue, taken from the Hong Kong Disneyland FY2025 accounts before depreciation, and corporate tax is 20 percent. The model gives NPV, IRR, payback, break-even capital cost and break-even attendance.

The base-case NPV is about THB -144 billion and the IRR is -5.2 percent. Nominal payback would take about 39 years, beyond the 20-year life, and discounted payback is never reached. The park would break even at a capital cost of about THB 50 billion, or at 43 million visits a year, which is 2.4 times the attendance of Magic Kingdom (17.8 million). Every scenario is negative: from -63 to -225 billion across capital cost, revenue and operating cost cases, and from -134 to -150 billion across a 30 to 40-year life and a 6 to 10 percent discount rate. We conclude that the proposal is not financially viable for an operator on these assumptions, and that capital cost and revenue scale matter more than project life or the discount rate. The result is provisional, because the capital cost is a news figure and replacement capital expenditure has no source.
